Do demand shifts matter for forecasting and stock?

Walmart M5, California (726 high-volume items, sales summed across stores), forecast evaluation 2015-12-07 to 2016-05-22, replicated in Texas and Wisconsin. M5 has no COVID-era data. Generated from outputs/results_facts.json.

Do demand shifts exist, and can we detect them?
Partlystrong evidence
An estimated 63.5% of items have a real shift (95% CI 59.8% to 67.2%), after removing chance alarms.
Caveat: The detector is coarse: a sudden 20% shift is caught only 18% of the time.
Which forecast is best?
LightGBMmoderate evidence
LightGBM: median error 0.744 vs 0.976 for the simple baseline (MASE; lower is better).
Caveat: It under-forecasts by 3.6% in total.
Does retraining after a shift help?
Nostrong evidence
None of 4 retraining methods improved forecasts. Simply restarting from the shift made error slightly worse (+0.0033 MASE, n = 488 items), and the same holds in 2 other states.
Caveat: Tested with one model (LightGBM) and four simple methods; small shifts are invisible to the detector.
Does a better forecast cut stock cost?
Yesstrong evidence
At a 95% target, LightGBM-based reorder points hold 16.6% less stock at 10.1% lower cost than the simple baseline; clearly lower in 3 of 3 time windows.
Caveat: Lead time and costs are assumptions, not measured data.
